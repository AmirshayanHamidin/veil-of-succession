# 10 Voice Questions for D1 (answer short; your answers become the style guide)

Read the three variants first. There are no wrong answers — these decide what
the final story bible (and every dialogue draft after it) sounds like.

1. **The reread test.** Which single paragraph across A/B/C did you reread or
   want to have written? Name it. That register is probably your center of
   gravity, even if you mix.

2. **Camera distance.** Should the bible (and the game's writing) live inside
   Yas's head (C), behind her shoulder (A), or in the case file about her (B)?
   Pick one as default; the others become seasoning.

3. **Persian on the page.** How much untranslated Farsi texture do you want —
   *Baba*, *agha*, *ta'arof*, *qanat*? None / a handful of anchor words /
   freely, with meaning clear from context?

4. **Yas's humor under pressure.** When things go wrong, does her wit get
   sharper (armor), disappear (the tell that she's scared), or turn on
   herself? This defines half her dialogue.

5. **Hossein.** Is he (a) a moral hero Yas is proud of, (b) a compromised
   idealist she's quietly angry at, or (c) a man she loves and fundamentally
   disagrees with? Variant C leans (c). This choice drives Q4 (confrontation)
   and Q6 (father/daughter beats).

6. **How violence reads.** A tranq dart vs. a kill: clinical and unremarked
   (B), a failure of craft (A), or a weight she carries — every guard is
   somebody's Shirazi (C)?

7. **Politics: foreground or weather?** Do you want the machinery on the page
   — council procedure, directorate names, tradecraft detail (B) — or politics
   as backdrop pressure on the people (C)?

8. **Sentimentality ceiling.** M0's "Give my regards to your wife" — is that
   line at your limit, comfortably inside it, or already too warm? Give one
   example line from any variant that crosses your line, if any does.

9. **Tense and person for the bible itself.** Present-tense pitch voice
   ("Yas is twenty-nine..."), past-tense storybook, or dossier headings (B)?
   This is about the document you'll actually maintain.

10. **The aftertaste.** "The succession is safe — for now." On the final
    screen, should that taste bitter (she lost more than she won), ambiguous
    (we don't know what she'll become), or defiant (worth it, and she'd do it
    again)? Feeds directly into Q5 (endings draft).
