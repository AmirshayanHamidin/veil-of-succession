# Variant B — le Carré Cold
*A work of political fiction. All persons, offices, and organizations are fictitious.*
*Register: institutional, clipped, procedural. Emotion exists but is filed under something else.*

---

## Opening

The office of Supreme Guide does not fall vacant. It transfers. The transfer
is governed by the Council of Custodians — twelve senior clerics and
officials, average age unrecorded out of politeness — and the Council's
deliberations are secret, which is the polite word for unwitnessed.

Ayatollah Mahdavi is dying. The medical bulletins say resting. The succession
process has therefore already begun, in the only way such processes begin: not
with a vote but with an inventory. Who owes what. Who fears what. Who can be
reached, and through whom.

General Farrokh Azadi, commander of the Guardian Corps intelligence
directorate, completed his inventory years ago. It exists as a physical
dossier — twelve files, one per Custodian, each a working summary of a life's
compromises — held in the palace where the Council convenes, on the principle
that the safest room is the one your victims must sit in. The general's
requirement is not the throne. Thrones attract attention. His requirement is
an emergency large enough to justify emergency powers: a foreign sabotage
operation, staged by his own directorate and attributed to the usual enemies,
timed to the Guide's death. A pliable cleric is elevated. The general
administers. The country calls it stability.

Against this stands an organization with no letterhead. Serving officers,
constitution-loyal, who noticed the inventory being taken and understood what
it was for. They style themselves The Qanat, after the old underground
channels — water moving beneath a surface that must never learn of it. The
Qanat runs agents. Its best is problematic by design.

## Core canon, in this register

**RAHIMI, Yasaman ("Yas"), 29.** Recruited abroad during her studies;
assessed as exceptional in signals and infiltration; retained despite — in
truth, because of — a conflict of interest no service would normally accept:
her father sits on the Council. The name Rahimi is her cover and her
vulnerability in the same breath. Palace gates open for the Custodian's
daughter without a document being checked; that is access no tradecraft
buys. The corresponding liability is written nowhere and known to everyone:
if she is taken, her father is not merely embarrassed. He is implicated,
tried, and hanged beside her. She is described by her handler as elegant,
controlled, and dry. All three are professional assets. None is the whole
file.

**RAHIMI, Hossein, Custodian.** Moderate. Suspects the Azadi operation;
cannot evidence it; declines exfiltration on the grounds that leaving the
institution concedes it. Unaware of his daughter's recruitment. This
unawareness is The Qanat's most closely held secret and its most fragile.

**AZADI, Farrokh, General, 58.** War record authentic, which is the
difficulty. Believes a strong hand is the republic's only viable form;
regards the constitution as sentiment. Soft-spoken in all recorded meetings.
The volume never rises. The files do.

**"DARYA."** Handler, voice-only, identity compartmented. Dry on the wire,
warm under it, never audibly afraid — which is a discipline, not an absence.

**Tone note.** Restraint throughout. No spectacle; spectacle is what Azadi
manufactures and what we are against. Violence is available and priced.
The victory condition is explicitly provisional: the succession is safe,
for now. In this trade, "for now" is the only tense that exists.
